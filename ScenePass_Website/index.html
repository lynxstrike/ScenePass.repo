Capabilities

The engine underneath it all

Everything above sits on top of the same battle-tested detection core — a consensus intro engine, three recap lanes, and a full deploy/upkeep pipeline, all running locally on your own PC.

Consensus intro engine

A pure-Python variance-map engine built on Chromaprint fingerprinting plus silence/black-frame fusion.

Three-lane recap detection

Falls through embedded subtitles, then Whisper speech-to-text, then Tesseract OCR on burned-in "Previously on" cards — whichever the episode actually has.

Confidence tiers, not guesses

Nothing deploys silently on shaky evidence. Uncertain calls sit in Hold until you confirm them, and every confirmation is logged.

Multi-format export

ScenePass JSON, Kodi/MPlayer EDL, and ffmpeg-style chapters, written side-by-side with your episodes — never into them.

Health scan

A read-only audit that catches orphaned sidecars, drift between the database and disk, and missing source files — with one-click fixes.

Self-installing dependencies

FFmpeg, fpcalc, VLC, Tesseract and Whisper models download themselves into ScenePass's own folder — nothing touches your system PATH.

Clean install, clean uninstall

A standard Windows setup that installs just for you, with no admin rights needed. It adds a Start-menu entry and removes itself cleanly from Apps & features.

Built-in updates

Checks this release channel for new versions, downloads and SHA-256-verifies the update, and stages it in place.

Three-layer disaster recovery

Restore the database from a backup, rebuild it straight from sidecar files, or reapply the gold ledger of every human-confirmed boundary — three independent safety nets, any one of which brings your library back.

Your confirmations are permanent

Once you confirm or hand-edit a boundary, it's locked. Simple and Force Scan alike reload it from cache instead of re-deriving it, so a re-scan can never quietly erase your work.

Effortless boundary review

Fine-tune any episode's boundaries with a built-in VLC preview — one at a time, or across a whole season at once with Bulk Review's fill-down and offset tools.

Pipeline

One pass through the library, six stages

Mount a folder, scan it, review what came back, and let ScenePass keep watching, scheduling, and sharing from there.

01 · Mount

Point ScenePass at a folder of shows. It reads the tree once and remembers it.

02 · Scan

The Consensus intro engine fingerprints audio across a season; a three-lane pipeline (subtitles → Whisper → OCR) catches recap cues.

03 · Review

Every result lands in a confidence tier — confident-deploy or verify-hold — so you know what to trust and what to eyeball.

04 · Export

Markers are written as sidecar files in whatever format your player reads. Your video files are never touched.

05 · Monitor & Schedule

A background watcher and an optional nightly window keep the library current without you lifting a finger.

06 · Share & Remote

Family profiles and a mobile remote bring the results to everyone in the house.

Before You Rely On It

Read this first

How it's built

ScenePass is a personal project: the concept, design decisions, and testing are the author's — every line of code is AI-written. There's no traditional dev team and no formal QA process; it's tested against a hand-graded benchmark set, not a commercial test matrix.

That's also why confidence tiers exist: the detection is heuristic (audio fingerprinting, speech-to-text, OCR), it will occasionally be wrong, and Hold status is the app telling you so rather than guessing silently.

No warranty

Provided "as is," with no warranty of accuracy, merchantability, or fitness for a particular purpose. ScenePass only ever reads and writes sidecar metadata next to your files — it never modifies, re-encodes, or deletes the video itself, and it doesn't download, rip, or circumvent DRM on anything.

  • Back up your library and any sidecar metadata before bulk scans or imports.
  • Review Hold-tier results before trusting them, especially on content you haven't manually confirmed.
  • The author isn't liable for data loss, mis-placed markers, or downstream effects in players that consume ScenePass's output.
